## Survey — what has been done

- **CONFIG_CHANGED never leaves the motherbee.** The router intercepts it before routing
  (`src/router/mod.rs`, local path and IRP path), **ignores the destination**, and copies it to all the
  local nodes and to the routers on the same host. There is no WAN forward. The docs
  (`01-arquitectura`, `02-protocolo`, `SY_nodes_spec`) say it reaches all the hives: it was never
  implemented (the WAN forward of broadcasts, 4a02e7b, was not plugged into this block).
- **`/jsr-opa-<hive>` is local**, and on a spoke nobody fills it. SY.opa.rules runs only on motherbee
  and worker (`system_nodes`); on ingress/egress it would not start: it waits for its ILK in the identity SHM, and
  there is no identity there. The Rust nodes already use the deterministic ILK (Model D'); the three Go ones do not.
- **Syncthing** moves only `dist/` and `blob/`. No config or policy file.
- **A 128 KiB cap per message** in the Rust and Go SDKs, with no splitting. A compiled OPA wasm weighs
  140–155 KB (`policy/system*.wasm`): **it does not fit in a message**.
- **What does cross hives:** LSA/topology (periodic), identity (full sync at start + deltas),
  the per-hive admin actions (`SY.config.routes@X`, `SY.wf-rules@X`), Syncthing.

## Bug confirmed live: `add_route` on a spoke overwrites the motherbee's routes

- **Initial state:** 0 routes on motherbee and worker1 (`config_version` 1). Admin counter at 0
  (`config_versions/` did not exist). Prior backup: `/root/sy-config-routes.yaml.pre-probe-20260930`.
- `POST /hives/worker1/routes {"prefix":"ZZPROBE1.","action":"DROP"}` → motherbee intact
  (the broadcast carried v1 and the motherbee was already at 1).
- `POST /hives/worker1/routes {"prefix":"ZZPROBE2.","action":"DROP"}` → **the motherbee ended up with
  ZZPROBE1. and ZZPROBE2.** `sy-config-routes` journal: `config changed applied payload_version=2`.
- **Cause:** after each add/remove, the admin broadcasts the list **of the target
  hive**; only the motherbee's SY.config.routes receives it, and since the admin's counter ended up
  one ahead of its own, it takes it as newer.
- **Cleanup:** I deleted the two routes on worker1 → both hives at 0 routes (versions 4 and 5).

## More bugs confirmed live with 0.1.35 (before the fix)

- **Any node could rewrite routes and command OPA.** `/root/rogue_node.py` on the motherbee: an
  arbitrary node (`AI.rogueprobe`) connects to the router and
  1. sends CONFIG_CHANGED `routes` → SY.config.routes answered `status ok, version 5` and installed
     `ZZROGUE1.`;
  2. sends `admin add_route` directly to `SY.config.routes@motherbee` → it installed `ZZROGUE2.`;
  3. sends `command rollback_policy` to `SY.opa.rules@motherbee` → it processed it (`NO_BACKUP`: it only
     failed because there was no backup).

  The CONFIG_CHANGED fan-out also reached AI.chat, SY.frontdesk.gov, SY.timer and SY.identity,
  which answered with errors. Cleanup: I deleted the two routes through the admin (motherbee at 0 routes,
  version 8).
- **On a worker, AI and cognition do not reach the vault.** Node `AI.vaultprobe@worker1`
  (`ai.generic`, `behavior.vault_key=openai_api_key`) + a user message from
  `/root/send_user.py` → it answers `missing_ai_api_key`; the router shows the lookup going to
  `SY.vault@worker1`, and SY.cognition@worker1 logs
  `vault unreachable: reason=NODE_NOT_FOUND original_dst=SY.vault@worker1`.
- **WF on a worker cannot publish.** `POST /hives/worker1/wf-rules` (workflow `w1probe`) →
  `PACKAGE_PUBLISH_FAILED: terminal transport response "UNREACHABLE"`: SY.wf-rules@worker1
  was publishing to `SY.admin@worker1`. It stayed registered without being published; I deleted it with
  `wf-rules/delete force`.

## Fixes (no design change) → 0.1.36

1. **Hive-scoped config.** `ConfigChangedPayload.hive` (SDK). The admin fills it in
   single-hive broadcasts (routes/VPN/taps after an add or remove, targeted OPA). SY.config.routes
   and SY.opa.rules **ignore another hive's config**. Routes are no longer overwritten, and an OPA clear
   aimed at worker1 no longer runs on the motherbee.
2. **Origin control** (security; today any node could rewrite its hive's routes, taps or
   OPA):
   - CONFIG_CHANGED becomes a **protected action**: the router stamps the socket's authenticated
     name and checks `system.rego` before the fan-out. The current rules already admit the two
     real senders (the admin of the same hive, the orchestrators), so **the wasm did not need
     recompiling**.
   - SY.config.routes: adds/removes of routes, VPN and taps **only from `SY.admin@motherbee`**
     (the same check as the orchestrator's F8).
   - SY.opa.rules: the `compile/apply/rollback_policy` commands **only from `SY.admin@motherbee`**.
3. **Nodes outside the motherbee pointed at services that do not exist on their hive:**
   - `VaultClient::for_primary` (SDK) in cognition, identity, ai-generic, ai-frontdesk-gov,
     io-slack, io-wapp and io-linkedhelper. Before, they went to `SY.vault@<their hive>`: on a worker, AI and
     cognition could not read secrets. SY.edge already handled it with its `vault_hive`.
   - SY.wf-rules publishes its packages via `SY.admin@motherbee` (before, `SY.admin@<its hive>`).
   - The `PRIMARY_HIVE_ID` constant moves to the SDK and the router re-exports it; in the Go SDK,
     `PrimaryHiveID` / `PrimaryAdminNode`.
4. **Identity: the replica now catches up on its own.** Each subscription to the delta stream starts
   with a full snapshot. Before, if the stream was cut, or if the worker started with the
   motherbee down, the replica stayed with old data (or only the system ILKs) **until the
   next restart**, even though the log promised to converge on reconnect. A sequence gap now
   reconnects and resyncs instead of `exit(0)`.

## Two more findings from the validation → 0.1.37

- **A WF's first spawn on a worker collides with Syncthing.** With the publish fixed, the
  deploy reaches the spawn: `RUNTIME_NOT_AVAILABLE: runtime 'wf.w1probe' not found in manifest` and,
  on retry, `version '0.0.2' not available`. The package takes ~11 s to reach worker1
  (measured: 20:53:11.6 on the motherbee, 20:53:23.1 on worker1). wf-rules tries only once.
  SY.wf-rules cannot ask for `SYSTEM_SYNC_HINT`/`SYSTEM_UPDATE` (they are `node_control_actions`, outside
  rule 4). **Fix:** while the orchestrator answers `RUNTIME_NOT_AVAILABLE` /
  `RUNTIME_NOT_PRESENT` / `BASE_RUNTIME_NOT_AVAILABLE`, wf-rules retries (backoff 1→4 s, up to
  20 s, within the admin's 30 s). On the motherbee the first attempt goes out just as before.
- **The SDK hid the vault's verdict.** `VaultClient::get` and `list` parsed the response as
  a success before looking at the `status`; the vault's error response only carries
  `status/error_code/message`, so a `KEY_NOT_FOUND` arrived as
  `json error: missing field key`. The `KEY_NOT_FOUND` branches of io-slack and io-wapp never
  ran. **Fix:** read the verdict from the raw JSON before parsing (a test with the real
  shape of the response).

## Under discussion: VPN, taps and static routes inside the user OPA

The operator: *"esto es evolución mal gerenciada y me encantaría unificar esto en OPA"* ("this is
badly managed evolution, and I would love to unify this in OPA"). Static
routes: rare, they can stay local. VPN: probably global (separate tenants, security;
a node on worker1 with one on worker2). Taps: the most used, it has to be global. Watch out: there are two OPAs
(user and system).

Proposal presented: one global policy with several decisions — `target` (exists), `taps` (on the
source router, a list of copies), `zone` (when the node registers; replaces VPN; zone = tenant,
possibly); static routes stay in SY.config.routes. The VPN/tap rules of
SY.config.routes and their admin actions go away. Main risk: the cost of evaluating wasm per message (taps).
Boundaries with the system OPA: taps never copy system messages; zones do not isolate system nodes
or the edge. Open: zone at registration vs `allow` per message; taps via wasm with a cache vs an
exported table; the default with no policy; hand-written rego vs generated from tables.

## OPA design on all hives — operator decisions

Basis: each hive keeps its copy and runs on its own (degraded if it loses contact); ingress/egress receive
OPA like everyone else; only the compiled output travels.

1. **SY.opa.rules runs on all hives**, ingress and egress included. Risk noted: whoever
   gets into a hive has the whole system's policy on disk.
2. **Transport: Syncthing, no polling.** *"no me gusta que tenga que andar preguntando… el
   syncthing me parece mejor, ya existe y va a todos los hives"* ("I don't like it having to keep asking
   around… syncthing seems better to me, it already exists and it goes to all the hives"). The motherbee
   compiles and leaves the compiled output in a synced folder; a broadcast announces the change; each hive
   loads it when it detects the file.
3. **Security: the simple option, no encryption for now.** A per-build hash in the notice and in the
   manifest; it loads only if it matches. Encryption with a key in the vault is left for later (proposal:
   encrypt only the copy that travels, because Syncthing runs as the `fluxbee` user).
4. **A single global policy**, the same on all hives/routers.
5. **Eventual convergence:** *"el sistema está pensado para que converja solo… y luego trabajar
   para que converja lo más rápido posible"* ("the system is meant to converge on its own… and then work
   so that it converges as fast as possible"). The admin answers when the motherbee has applied it, with the
   state per hive.
6. **Global `PUT /config/routes`:** under discussion (almost closed).

**Closed later (operator):** config-routes does not go into OPA. *"arranca con OPA y guarda el
trabajo sobre config-routes (VPN global via LSA me suena lo correcto)… si se borra hive se pierden
las rutas"* ("start with OPA and shelve the work on config-routes (a global VPN via LSA sounds right to
me)… if a hive is deleted, the routes are lost") → backlog A-18. `PUT /config/routes|vpns|taps` ships with
OPA: it is a prerequisite for CONFIG_CHANGED to cross hives.

## Global OPA built → 0.1.41 (00:00–00:45 UTC on Oct 1)

What was built follows the decisions above; the canonical description is
[`docs/opa-distribution.md`](../../docs/opa-distribution.md).

- SY.opa.rules runs on the 4 hives and derives its ILK from its name (it does not need identity, which ingress and
  egress do not have). Only the motherbee's compiles: it applies and publishes the wasm, never the rego, in
  `dist/policy/opa/` (manifest + `policy-<hash16>.wasm`).
- Syncthing folder `fluxbee-dist-policy` for all roles, `sendonly` on the motherbee. It inherits
  the `vendor` peers, so it reaches already joined hives without redoing the join.
- Each hive installs what was published at startup, on the notice and every 5 s (a local check, nobody asks
  anything), only if the sha256 matches the manifest. It keeps running with its last policy if
  cut off.
- The admin writes globally; after applying on the motherbee it notifies `SY.opa.rules@*` with `CONFIG_CHANGED {opa, sync}`
  and answers who already runs it and who is still pending.
- CONFIG_CHANGED stops being a local delivery that ignored the destination: it travels through normal routing,
  crosses hives and is a protected SYSTEM action.

**Migration of 8.x** (a new recipe in HANDBOOK §12, because a spoke's `hive.yaml` is not
re-emitted): the motherbee's `hive.yaml` before the `apt install` → the binary reaches ingress1/egress1
in under a minute → the `hive.yaml` of those two → core-update to the three. Details in the ledger.

**Live validation:**

- Global apply (v6): the 4 hives and their routers with the same policy in 16 s.
- Catching up: with egress1's Syncthing cut by nft, v7 answered `pending: [egress1]` and
  egress1 stayed on v6; on unblocking, it installed it on its own, in 27 s.
- Clear: the motherbee cleared at once; the spokes, after 63 s. The motherbee's Syncthing events
  show why: the applies were scanned after 10 s, the clear after 60 s (only
  renames and deletions, which the watcher holds until its timeout) → A-19.
- Security: a write for a single hive → 400; `/var/lib/fluxbee/opa` `700` and SHM `600` on
  ingress1/egress1. Reviewing who can send CONFIG_CHANGED now that it crosses hives: the router
  also admits the orchestrators, and SY.opa.rules and SY.config.routes applied whatever reached
  them → A-20.
- Factory reset in dry-run: it sees the policy of the 4 hives.
