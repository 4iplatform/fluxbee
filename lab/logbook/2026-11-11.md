## 0.1.132: the router alone

- **The branch:** `coa/router-131` (A-245, A-195 and their reviews) went on as `coa/router-132`
  with A-208, A-247, A-246, A-253's line and A-265; its review found two low items, fixed once
  (each id in the named line quoted, escaped and cut at 128 bytes, its 10 a window by the alarm's
  interval; the stale check rebuilds the FIB too), and two more recorded (A-268, two FIB builds at
  once; A-269, a node name taken and logged as it comes). Rebased onto daily with every subject at
  `[0.1.131]`, CI green on the branch and on the integrated head. `ops.py health` now counts the
  router's lines only when the pattern opens the message, so a quoted id cannot add to `streamlost`.
- **The baseline first:** the gate's runs on 0.1.131, before the deploy. A sender on ingress1 that
  connected 1 s before sending to a worker1 node got all its 2,600 frames dropped at worker1 as
  `foreign_source`, unanswered (A-195); with both known, worker1 refused 1,536 `LINK_BUSY` that never
  reached the sender (A-245); a new node on worker1 was reached by name from ingress1 only after
  5.98 s (A-195's beacon, A-265).
- **The deploy:** build 23 min, publish 16 s, deploy 288 s, health clean. The spokes settled by their
  own records 37 to 42 s after the update's call (each core restart took its router down with it);
  SY.admin closed the update 41.9 s after it opened it.
- **The gate on 0.1.132:** the same runs: the new sender reached worker1, all 1,536 `LINK_BUSY` reached
  it on ingress1, and the new node was reached by name 0.33 s after its connect, with no
  `NODE_NOT_FOUND` from the hub. The gateway's broadcast answer is unchanged (one `LINK_BUSY` with a
  target's link full, none otherwise, each target once). A-253: a 100 KB message named once; a 6 KB
  forged `thread_id` escaped and cut in a 494-byte line, no forged line; 15 at once, 10 named and the
  alarm's *"did not name 5"*; `streamlost=17` in health, the alarms' own count.
- **Found by the gate:** A-272. The baseline's broadcast run showed the motherbee dropping its own
  node's broadcast as `foreign_source` from ingress1 and worker1: every router sends a WAN broadcast
  back to the peer it came from, and the hub sends a spoke's broadcast back to that spoke. That is
  most of the `foreign_source` lines A-195 counted. The fix (split horizon) is the router's: its next
  release, alone.
