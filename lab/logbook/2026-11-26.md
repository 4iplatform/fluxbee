## Stage 2's build design

- **The read:** five readers went through the code for it, one area each:
  - SY.admin and the WF precedent;
  - the orchestrator's node lifecycle;
  - io.wapp and the IO family's inbound;
  - the AI SDK's reply and the router;

  The facts that shape the design are its §0.
- **The design:** [digital-executive-v1-stage-2](../../docs/digital-executive-v1-stage-2.md), a draft.
  - SY.deploy is a Rust node on the motherbee, with files, one deployment at a time.
  - The plain ED's source ships inside it.
  - A birth is nine steps through SY.admin, journaled with ids minted before the first. A retry
    converges.
  - The admission is bounded by a reserved namespace of names and the launcher's stamp.
  - The conscious speaks as the ED from its config, and a `hand` channel on the ED tells the router.
  - The IO stamps its person as `dst_ilk` from `io.person_ilk`.
  - The orchestrator gets a retire mode.
  - Three releases, then `enforce`.

## The operator's first answers

- **Decided:**
  - **call 1:** the plain ED's source ships inside SY.deploy;
  - **call 2:** the conscious is born with its definition, and the native texts carry no line on
    whether the ED is an AI;
  - **call 4:** Cloud's four operations in this stage. The operator: SY.deploy will deploy every
    solution, not only EDs, so it must not be built so narrow that the next ones mean a lot of new
    code.
- **Applied to the design for call 4:** the orders are about deployments (`cd_deploy`, `cd_get`,
  `cd_list`, `cd_remove`), and an ED's birth is a deployment of `plain-ed` into a new ED grouping.
  The records are groupings and deployments. One source format and one step builder serve every
  source. The admission's namespace is any grouping's, and its clause admits whole classes of
  actions; only purges and what the whole installation shares stay out.
- **Explained again, and waiting:** call 3 (the hand, now with an example in its §8) and call 5
  (`enforce` right after 2C's gate, in its §11).

## The operator's second answers

- **Call 5, rejected:** no `enforce`, and no security mechanism before the flow it protects is
  built. A panel's requirements are talked one by one, in detail.
- **Call 3, reopened from the bottom:** the operator does not want the hand as a channel, and asks
  what a channel is, why messages carry `dst_ilk`, and whether stage 1's direction on the router is
  right.
- **Call 4, one more piece:** a catalog of the packages ready to deploy and a tree per tenant for
  Cloud's screen. Who keeps the catalog (Archi, or SY.deploy fed by Archi) is to agree.

## The operator's third answers: clean first, then identity from the bottom

- **On the talks:** they weighed the rework, not the consequences on the process. From now on every
  design decision shows its consequences (who reads and writes what, what bends later).
- **Call 4:** Archi creates and publishes the packages, and SY.deploy learns what Archi has, with no
  polling. Proposed: an event through SY.admin, with SY.deploy keeping its own immutable copy.
- **Call 3:** the security path bent the design's hardest topic. The relation between a node and
  its ED belongs in identity and its SHM, built once and properly. Before anything else, what was
  built for the wrong path comes out of the code. The inventory goes to the operator first.
