## Case 4, decided with the operator

A design conversation, in chat, one problem at a time: handing the conversation to a person of the
team. Everything is in §5.4.

- **The WF regulates and owns nothing** (§5.4.1): it decides only what its code fixes, never writes
  an LLM's content, and never owns a conversation. The facts travel in the message, and each node
  decides with them.
- **The subject mark** (§5.4.2): the WF says `new`, `same` or `end`, and io.slack places the
  message. A case keeps one Slack thread.
- **The first post** (§5.4.3): sales' summary as written, and the person's facts.
- **A menu for people** (§5.4.4): io.slack shows it as a button, the click comes back as a fact,
  and the team member's note reaches sales in their own words.
- **Two timers** (§5.4.5): "nobody took it" and "quiet", the WF's fixed rules. The operator: *"las
  reglas son cosas que se tiene que programar en el wf"*.
- **Nothing from an IO ends in silence** (§5.4.6): `NOT_TAKEN`, with a reason, which io.slack shows
  to whoever wrote.
- **`answer_team`** (§5.4.7): a supervisor who asks sales something gets the answer in the
  consultation's thread.
- **The catalog of known options** (§5.4.8), the operator's idea: *"un DEF para todo lo conocido y
  algo que sea a gusto del usuario"*. Declared once in the language; a definition names a known
  option by its name alone.
- **The operator, on the build:** *"te dejo planear el trabajo a vos, solo recordá esto, fluxbee
  hoy es versión alpha no expuesta a clientes... es mejor romper ahora que dejar para después y no
  hay backcompatibilidad"*.
- **Two topics of their own, after the track:** A-137 (which conversations cognition analyzes, and
  how much, when every interlocutor is an agent; the operator's idea is a mark in the message) and
  A-138 (the conversation's trace in the message: *"ya veo la herramienta completa en el mensaje
  para ver con el trail cómo funcionó o dónde falló"*).

## io.slack: only what the WF's test needs

The plan first took in io.slack's open findings (A-130, A-108, A-129). The operator:

- *"cuidado con considerar que io.slack va a funcionar como lo está hoy... lo que no quiero es
  abrir mil frentes, solo para terminar la prueba sobre los casos de WF no quería cambiar el
  IO.slack ahora"*.
- On A-129's plan, a relay of Slack events between io.slack nodes: *"parecería un workaround"*.
  The test does not need it, so it went.
- **The definition for io.slack's revamp:** each Slack channel is one ICH and one node, or else one
  node per workspace that multiplexes its channels. Over Socket Mode, one node per channel with one
  Slack app cannot work; over Slack's HTTP events it can, through the edge's fan-out (io.wapp's
  model). Recorded in FINDINGS A-129.
- **The order after the track,** the operator: *"apenas salimos de esto de WF y revisamos el io.web,
  encaramos los nodos IO completos... empezamos por io.slack"*.

## The review round

Five reviewers, each blind to the others' lens: the engine, the language, io.slack, security, and
the example with the docs. Everything they found is in §5.4.11. What mattered most:

- **The example lost a supervisor's second message,** and a person's message during an evaluation
  dropped the consultation. That was Case 3's logic; `answer_team` makes it likelier.
- **A timer the WF cancelled could still fire,** and send a taken case back as "nobody took it".
- **On a thread several people share,** a WF's own messages carried the first writer's identity
  (A-136 seen again).
- **io.slack's read loop** could make a click's modal miss its 3 seconds behind a file.
- **Case 1's invoicing took the team's `handback`;** the catalog gained `done`.

Two agents fixed the Go and io.slack sides; the docs were mine. The local CI passed again, 25 of
25.
