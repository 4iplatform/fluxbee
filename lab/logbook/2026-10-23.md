# 2026-10-23 — io.web v2: the direction, and the door ready for a second host (0.1.84)

Where the track stands is in [io-web-v2](../../docs/io-web-v2.md); this page only tells how it got
there. The facts of the release are in the ledger's
[0.1.84 entry](../DEPLOYMENTS.md).

## How it went

- **2026-10-22, evening.**
  - Three read-only surveys of the code mapped:
    - the blob and IO.blob;
    - how the edge publishes;
    - io.web's history.
  - A first draft, *edge-publishing-v1*, extended the artifact circuit to sites, uploads through
    the edge and an inbox from the ingress to the core. Two reviews hardened it.
  - The operator stopped it: files move only through Syncthing, nodes carry references, the edge
    publishes URLs.
  - It was never committed. What it proposed is listed as withdrawn in io-web-v2 §8, and its
    reviews' lessons in §7.
- **2026-10-23, a conversation without agents.**
  - **What is needed:** Archi as a URL for Fluxbee Cloud, and artifacts. There is no upload path:
    files come through their channels, and Archi's page does its own uploads.
  - **What was missing:** the edge's third surface, the real reverse proxy of the io.web beta,
    was never built. Today the edge relays channel messages (64 KiB, JSON only) and serves
    single-file artifacts.
  - **Agreed:**
    - one door (the edge, a reverse proxy) and several managers (IO nodes, each with its own
      security);
    - io.web behind the proxy, on the motherbee next to Archi, reached over an internal HTTP
      channel with mutual TLS (the mesh socket cannot carry web traffic);
    - artifacts served by the edge from its Syncthing copy;
    - no manager on the ingress, and no new hive.
  - **How to continue (the operator):** treat io.web as a first-class citizen of every leg of
    Fluxbee, not as a security problem only, and build from the outside in, the skeleton first.

- **The skeleton** (io-web-v2 §11) was designed, then reviewed once for fit and once for
  security. The review split it into two releases:
  - 0.1.84 gets the certificate for two names, alone with the door's own fixes, since it can take
    `/e/` down;
  - 0.1.85 gets the proxy and io.web.
- **A-87 and A-88,** found by the security review, are defects of the edge as it runs today.
- **DNS:** the operator created the CNAME `archi.hive-k3m9x7q2.fluxbee.ai`. It resolves through
  public resolvers.

- **Release 0.1.84** (io-web-v2 §11.1):
  - built by four agents in their own worktrees, one per area:
    - the edge;
    - SY.admin (ACME, the web routes, A-81);
    - the router and the policy;
    - storage;
  - one code review round, whose fixes went to the edge and SY.admin; then deployed to the 4 hives
    in 266 s;
  - the operator approved the certificate's transition, the four steps of §11.1. Since 23:19 UTC
    the edge serves one Let's Encrypt certificate for both names.
- **The gate** found nothing to fix. What took thought:
  - **A-81 without the model.** The first try used a real agent in a test tenant and failed with
    "missing api key": a tenant's agents read only their own tenant's AI key, never root's. That
    is the design. The test then took the agent's path without the model:
    - a mesh client, carrying the test agent's identity on worker1;
    - the page written into the blob;
    - the publication asked 1 s later.
  - **A false negative in the gate's own script.** Python's text mode turned the `\r\n` of the
    response into `\n`, so the body check failed while the page was right. A second run, with
    fresh content so the wait was exercised again, checked the body and the 404 after the
    unpublish.
  - **The deletion of the first run's blob** reached the motherbee only after about a minute
    (Syncthing delays deletions). By the end of the second run, nothing was left on any hive.
